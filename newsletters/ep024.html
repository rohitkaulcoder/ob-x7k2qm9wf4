A niche priced as a platform

Oura is expected to go public this week. The Information's Martin Peers puts its fiscal 2025 revenue at $907.9 million, more than twice the prior year, and says the next nine months grew 74%. It generated about $100 million of cash in fiscal 2025. Real sales. Real cash. The harder question is what those numbers are worth when a premium wearable finds its natural ceiling.

At the midpoint of a tentative price range, Peers estimates a valuation near nine times current-year revenue if recent growth holds through the fourth quarter. GoPro reached a similar sales multiple in its heyday. Its revenue peaked around $1.6 billion in 2015, then fell to $651 million in 2025 as phone cameras improved. Oura is not GoPro. The comparison is about the danger of extrapolating a distinctive hardware use case into a permanent platform.

A ring is comfortable for sleep tracking, and a subscription can turn daily signals into a continuing relationship. But the ring begins around $349, and the buyer has alternatives: watches, wrist trackers and phones. The numbers that decide the thesis are retention after two years, paid-subscription renewal, replacement cycles, gross margin after returns, and whether data improves the product enough that a rival cannot simply match the sensor. A niche can be valuable. Nine times sales prices it as something larger.

A chip with two gatekeepers

China's industry ministry has asked ByteDance, Alibaba and other companies how many Nvidia RTX Pro 5500 chips they want and how they would use them. Officials have signaled that some purchases may be approved, The Information reports. No approval date, quantity or standard is settled. The chip's status under US export controls has not been publicly resolved either. One buyer needs permission at both ends of the route.

The RTX Pro 5500 is a professional-workstation GPU, not Nvidia's top data-centre accelerator. It lacks the fast inter-chip links and expensive high-speed memory that help large clusters train models or serve very large ones. Buyers still want it for inference, the work of running trained models for users. ByteDance is weighing roughly one million units; Nvidia reportedly aims to ship about 500,000 per quarter to China from late December. At that target rate, the contemplated ByteDance volume alone is two quarters of supply. Both figures remain plans subject to policy and production.

Chinese chip buyers put its price at roughly 85,000 to 90,000 yuan, about $13,000, comparable to Huawei's Ascend 950PR. A million times $13,000 is $13 billion in indicative chip value, not booked Nvidia revenue. Beijing has restricted Nvidia to support domestic suppliers and cites security concerns Nvidia denies. Washington restricts the most advanced exports. Nvidia's China data-centre revenue is absent from its forecast this quarter. The investment angle is not just sales: keeping Chinese developers on Nvidia's software stack may slow a complete shift to Huawei. But an option on market access is not a shipment.

Oil money, venture terms

New Mexico's $75 billion sovereign wealth fund has committed around $2 billion to VC managers over three years as oil and gas revenues rose. Before 2022 it required recipient funds to invest a portion in startups headquartered in-state with at least half their operations there. Dropping the binding rule opened the door to more national managers. Some now make a nonbinding pledge to consider the state for portfolio activity.

More than half the commitments arrived since early 2025. The fund's three-year net internal rate of return is reported at 32.7%, but The Information says it is unclear how much is realized cash versus paper startup markups. A private fund can mark a holding up without distributing a dollar to the public treasury. The investment is meant to diversify the state's finite oil wealth and support public services; a paper IRR cannot pay a teacher.

There is some evidence of local activity. UP.Partners received $80 million in total, invested $45 million of one tranche across about 18 startups and opened an Albuquerque office. One funded startup, Pacific Fusion, began construction on a $1 billion campus in the state. That is a concrete project, not proof every portfolio company will move. The policy trade is familiar to Indian state innovation funds: a local-deployment mandate may push managers into weaker deals, while removing it leaves local jobs uncertain. Measure financial distributions and verified local investment separately.

Payments arrive one merchant at a time

Mostly Metrics describes a US beach town where even old cash-only businesses now accept phone taps. The change did not arrive as one national launch. Card readers got cheap, small merchants joined the network, and each little friction lost its reason to persist. The beach-bike rental, the market stall and the split dinner bill are a product roadmap in miniature.

Financial startups then use a narrow first job to learn the next one. Chime targeted bank fees for a particular group. Wealthfront noticed customers wiring money to escrow companies and moved into mortgages. Robinhood began with free trading and expanded into a larger menu. Not all expansion works; the point is that observed customer behavior is a better guide than a generic claim to be a financial super-app.

India already has a broad payment interface in UPI. The next valuable layer is often behind the tap: reconciliation, fraud, credit, collections and the workflow around a transaction. Owning the payment screen is not the same as owning the economics.

The rails have owners

Fintech Business Weekly reports that Bridge, Stripe's stablecoin infrastructure arm, and Lead Bank support US virtual-account programs serving Venezuelan users through a temporary humanitarian sanctions license after earthquakes. Bridge's public documentation still lists Venezuela as a prohibited jurisdiction. That mismatch calls for clarification about the exact program and license, not an unsupported accusation of wrongdoing. Augustus, a bank with conditional US charter approval but not yet open, appears to be preparing business accounts there.

A virtual-account app displays US account details to a user. A bank receives a deposit; an infrastructure provider routes and records it; the app credits a sub-account. Each handoff carries questions about identity, sanctions screening and who bears loss when something goes wrong. A relief license for a defined purpose is not a blank cheque for every customer and transaction. Growth in processed volume can disguise risk that appears months later as frozen accounts or a lost banking partner. The real product is the rails plus accountable controls.

Source: Fintech Business Weekly, Sep 27, 2026

Oil is a startup cost

Bloomberg's Asia briefing says Iran has held to a seven-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz after the US rejected its conditions. Negotiations may resume this week. A proposal is not an agreement, and a single diplomatic headline is not a precise oil-price forecast.

For India, disruption risk can work through imported crude into the currency, inflation and fiscal room. For startups it works through the gross margin of logistics, delivery, aviation, chemicals and power-heavy production. The useful question is not whether a founder follows geopolitics. It is whether the business still works if fuel costs move before prices to customers can change. Bloomberg also notes Japan converting old golf courses to solar sites, a reminder that energy demand alone does not create land, grid access and permits.
