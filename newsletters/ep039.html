The frontier is becoming a niche

Meta unveiled Muse Wonder on Monday and said it can rival OpenAI and Anthropic in areas including coding, finance and law. Google, OpenAI and Anthropic have each made similar claims in the past six weeks. Meta's comparison with OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Fable comes from Meta's own tests, as reported by The Wall Street Journal. It has not been independently checked in the sources read.

The more interesting number is on the buyer's side. The Information reports that only 14% of AI model usage on OpenRouter ran through the ten highest-quality models in the third quarter, down from 55% in the first quarter of last year. OpenRouter is a marketplace: a developer writes code once, and requests can go to many models, billed per token. The data shows what developers choose when each choice has a price.

Illustrative arithmetic, not real price lists. Take a company using two billion tokens a day. At $15 per million tokens, all of it on a top model costs $30,000 a day. At $0.50, all of it on a cheap model costs $1,000. Send 14% to the top model and 86% to the cheap one and the bill is $4,200 plus $860, about $5,060, an 83% cut. The OpenRouter figure is a market share, not one firm's routing split, but the logic holds.

This is not really about which lab has the best model. It is about who wins the invoice. If only a small slice of traffic needs the premium tier, the premium tier is a niche. The volume sits in the cheap tier, where margins are thin and rivals are many.

The buildout runs on other people's money

Microsoft announced Maia 300, its latest in-house AI chip, and said it plans multigigawatt deployments starting next year. A gigawatt is a billion watts, roughly one large power plant. A chip designed for your own workload can lower cost per token, which matters more if customers are moving to cheaper models.

Bloomberg's Odd Lots newsletter points to two charts. Bloomberg Intelligence's estimate of installed data center gigawatts has jumped since May. Its data center copper demand estimate roughly triples on an annual basis in coming years, with copper near record highs. Separately, Renaissance Macro's Neil Dutta shows supplier delivery times in regional manufacturing surveys at a post-pandemic record. When delivery times stretch, buyers compete and suppliers can raise prices.

Who pays? The Information notes that Bain Capital's Bridge Data Centres is in talks to raise a Malaysia loan from $2.8 billion to about $6.6 billion, citing Bloomberg. That would be one of the largest data center debt deals in Asia. Illustrative math: at an assumed 7% rate, $6.6 billion costs about $462 million a year in interest. A lender wanting 1.4 times coverage needs roughly $650 million of yearly cash flow. That cash comes from tenants renting capacity, so the lender is underwriting demand and price for years.

Joe Weisenthal's counterpoint is that AI keeps improving, unlike the late-1990s telecom build. The tension with the first section is price. Volume can grow while price per token falls. Bloomberg's Big Take headline on a circular funding model drawing foreign cash was seen only as a headline and summary, so it is not analysed here.

In India, distribution may beat the model

Invoice AI's issue 41 reports from ElevenLabs' first India summit. It lists over 250 enterprise clients, 26,000 monthly API users, 100 million yearly conversations on its agent platform, 20 million customers reached, 70% of interactions in local languages, and 2,700 voices from 700 voice actors. The CEO earlier said the company wants $100 million of annual recurring revenue in India. Nobody mentioned that number at the summit.

The details are more telling than the totals. India GM Karthik Rajaram described early use cases such as EMI renewal calls, now moving toward harder customer service. A Hinglish toggle packages work that used to take many rounds of prompting and configuration with forward-deployed engineers. ElevenLabs is also hiring engineers in India for its global product, which the newsletter calls unusual at its scale.

It signed an MoU with Karnataka on voice AI for government services. Sarvam's total funding of $387 million is less than the $500 million ElevenLabs raised in one round this year. The newsletter says that in India you cannot win on models alone, you need a sales machine, and ElevenLabs is building one.

The implication is not that Indian model builders cannot win. It is that if models get cheaper, the moat moves to language data, enterprise trust and distribution. The open question the newsletter raises is protocol: how agents call people and other agents. Sierra and Decagon named ElevenLabs among partners, and Vapi is building agent-calling infrastructure.

A 2021 IPO goes private at a 90% discount

Bumble is in talks to go private at about $1 billion, the Journal reports. The stock rose 50%, or $1.26, to $3.80. The IPO priced at $43, and the stock nearly doubled early, valuing it at $9.5 billion. The Journal says shares are down more than 90% since. The buyer is Ithaca Holdings, founded recently by two former Silicon Valley executives, former Sequoia partner Ravi Gupta and Meta's former revenue chief John Hegeman, per The Information. The deal could come this week if talks do not fall apart.

The Information's frame is the right one: the IPO class of 2021 is being taken off the market, often far below where it entered. Growth slowed in a crowded category. For investors who rely on public-market exits, the lesson is that listing is not the same as lasting value.

Retail is trading like 2021 again

The Journal reports options trading in individual stocks has returned to meme-stock levels, about $700 billion a day, despite conflict, rising rates and $100 oil. This time the story is AI, solid earnings and large IPOs, not AMC and GameStop.

An option is the right to buy or sell at a set price by a set date. Illustrative: a $2 contract on 100 shares risks $200 against $5,000 of stock at $50. If the stock reaches $55, a contract set at $50 is worth about $500 at expiry. If it does not move, the $200 is gone. That asymmetry draws crowds in rising markets.

Put this next to Bumble. Last cycle's names are being collected cheaply while this cycle's enthusiasm is back. That does not prove a top. It does show that mood and value are different things.

A market that predicts a court may influence it

Matt Levine's Money Stuff covers Kalshi's sports contracts. Courts disagree. New Jersey, which lost one case, has asked the Supreme Court to review it, and 39 states and the NFL backed that request. Levine cannot estimate the odds, so he reaches for Kalshi, which lists no such contract. Joseph Grundfest proposed that it should.

Levine's puzzle is reflexivity. If that market showed 90%, would the number change what lawyers or judges do? People worry about the same in election markets, and the Journal noted juries. A forecast can become one input to the thing it forecasts.
