The agencies landed

Thursday morning this show said the rating agencies were circling the AI trade. They had already landed. S&P placed Nvidia’s credit rating on CreditWatch Negative on Wednesday, citing the resignation-and-reinstatement of Jensen Huang as insufficient answer to questions about the “effectiveness of Nvidia’s management and governance framework, including board independence and ‘key man’ risk,” plus China exposure. CreditWatch means a formal review and a possible downgrade within 90 days. The rating is still AA, two steps from the top, earned the old way: $44 billion of free cash flow last quarter. But that rating is load-bearing for the whole AI financing stack - Nvidia’s credit supports customer debt, OpenAI included, and a downgrade into a December rate hike reprices that machine twice. The mechanical point: bond mandates and index rules key off ratings, so a downgrade forces mechanical selling and raises the coupon on every future dollar the build-out borrows against Nvidia’s name. Context: the SOX remains down 17% since the dismissal news broke, -22% at Wednesday’s worst - the market reinstated the man and kept selling the sector. Monday, Huang testifies behind closed doors before three House committees. S&P just handed everyone in that room a fresh exhibit.

Half the memory, a third of the cost

The more consequential story of the week arrived as a benchmark. SemiAnalysis put Amazon’s Trainium3 on InferenceX and the headline is a sentence nobody expected this year: it beats Nvidia’s GB300 on the metric that matters, cost to generate tokens. At 135 tokens per second per user, Trainium3 with vLLM yields a 73.5% inference gross margin against 53% for GB300 with SGLang, at three-year rental prices. The how is the interesting part. Trainium3 has 17% of Blackwell Ultra’s theoretical flops and 61% of its memory bandwidth, but inference splits into compute-hungry prefill and memory-hungry decode, and Amazon delivers bandwidth at roughly 30% of Nvidia’s TCO. The architecture is the tell: every Nvidia generation since 2017 bolted another AI organ onto a graphics chip (Volta’s tensor cores, Hopper’s TMA, Blackwell’s tensor memory), while Trainium was designed from a blank page - tiles as a first-class primitive, descriptor engines that hit peak memory bandwidth at small message sizes where Nvidia’s hardware cannot, and a profiler Anthropic’s head of performance publicly calls far better than Nvidia’s. The “Short King” detail: 144GB of HBM per chip, half of GB300, because extra memory only pays if it buys throughput at the target latency. The one counterweight is Nvidia’s own Vera Rubin, the only accelerator ahead across the whole curve. This is not “Nvidia is beaten.” It is that the inference layer, where tokens actually get sold, is now a price war against a rival with Amazon’s supply chain. Google’s TPU trades the same blows on the same benchmark, so call it a merchant-silicon trend, not an Amazon anomaly.

The midday rescue

Governments that want stability issue statements after the close. Governments that want a rally issue them at lunch. China’s State Council chose lunch on Friday: a bigger role for the “national team” of state funds, policies to channel long-term money into equities, a fraud crackdown, and a push for dividends and buybacks. The CSI 300 jumped more than 4%, its best day since October 2024; the chip-heavy Star 50 surged 15% in a session; Hang Seng Tech rose more than 6%; copper had its best day since January. South Korea follows Monday: officials meet the major financial companies, with an expansion of the $8 billion stabilization fund on the table. Three weeks of AI-doubt wobble, and governments across Asia are openly backstopping equity markets. The uncomfortable version of the same chart: the interventions arrive after three weeks of wobble in markets still up enormously on the year. If this is the pain threshold, the put is shallower than anyone modeled.

Aladdin’s private markets

BlackRock is rebuilding Aladdin for the Total-Portfolio Approach - one risk view across public and private holdings - and the numbers frame the industry’s direction. Unlisted assets are forecast to top $34 trillion by 2031. BlackRock bought eFront and Preqin for $3.2 billion in March 2025 and is integrating both; Aladdin was about 10% of the firm’s $24 billion revenue last year, and management wants technology and private markets at 30% of revenue by 2030. Aladdin is the risk-analytics operating system much of the institutional world already runs its public books on, so extending it into privates is not a new product - it is an invasion of adjacent territory with the existing customer base as the beachhead. For a VC the implication is quiet but large: the exit environment for private assets is being productized. The buyer of your Series C position in 2029 may be an Aladdin dashboard.

Bloomberg, Going Private, “BlackRock Supercharges Aladdin for the Private Markets Era,” Sinead Cruise, Nov 27 (newsletter text as delivered).

The ladder gets its missing rung

India’s deep-tech capital stack had its third strong day of the week, and the shape matters more than the items. CortexSpark Ventures launched Viksit Bharat Fund-I with a ₹1,000 crore (~$108 million) target plus greenshoe, roughly 20 startups across space, defense, robotics, AI, bio-AI and energy, chaired by Manoj Kohli (ex-Bharti Airtel CEO, SoftBank India). Oister Global launched a ₹600 crore (~$64.5 million) deep-tech growth fund aimed explicitly beyond seed. Green Aero raised a $14 million Series A to build indigenous jet engines around its Blue Dragon core - a club with essentially four members, and a Bengaluru startup is attempting it. And Israel Aerospace Industries graduated its second NEUSPHERE accelerator cohort in India: four startups across GeoAI, maritime intelligence, quantum RF sensing and quantum key distribution. Seed funds, two new growth funds, foreign defense primes sourcing at the accelerator stage - the missing rung was always the $20-50 million check between prototype and procurement, and this week’s funds aim squarely at it. If you passed on Indian space and defense two years ago because there was no follow-on capital, that excuse expired this week.

IndiaTechReport, Nov 27 (CortexSpark, Oister, NEUSPHERE, Green Aero; full newsletter text).

Quick hits and the calendar

SpaceX got FCC approval for up to one million satellites for orbital AI data centers - ambitious, unproven, but the paperwork is real. Singapore is in talks with Washington to retain AI-chip access. Trump will personally lead the TSMC Arizona groundbreaking. Apple pulled MiniMax’s chatbot over sexualized content. SAP’s churn hit eleven executive-board departures in seven years, with two of six seats about to sit empty - “headless,” a contact joked, which is also the name of Salesforce’s new AI tools. ElevenLabs is in early talks at a $33 billion valuation, up sharply from its September tender (the multiple-of-eleven gimmick intact). Newcomer scoops Benchmark in talks for a second defense-tech deal in two months plus a rare bio investment (paywalled; headline treatment). Clouded Judgement’s Thanksgiving note frames the founder psychology - do too little and competitors pass you, do too much and you die of complexity - against the numbers: median cloud software at 4.4x forward revenue, top five at 27.6x, high-growth at 11.7x, with the 10-year at 4.9%. AI multiples have compressed hard toward the historic mean, which is what a market pricing a December hike looks like. Calendar: Monday, Huang’s closed-door testimony and Korea’s stabilization meeting; December 9, the Fed decision, blackout now in effect; and the S&P clock - 90 days from Wednesday to a possible Nvidia downgrade.
