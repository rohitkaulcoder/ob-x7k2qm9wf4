A revenue correction, not an income statement

The Information corrects OpenAI's reported annualized revenue from $70bn to closer to $50bn, following Financial Times reporting. It says the earlier figure came from mistaken calculations. This is a correction to the public picture, not evidence that $20bn vanished from audited annual revenue.

Annualized revenue commonly multiplies a recent month by twelve. It is a conditional pace, not trailing twelve-month revenue or necessarily subscription-backed annual recurring revenue. Cloud-provider cuts can further complicate comparisons between firms. A gross figure and a retained-revenue figure answer different questions.

The investment consequence is wider than one valuation multiple: expected AI revenue supports forecasts of infrastructure demand. Revenue, cash available for investment and future chip purchases are separate steps. Preserve the definition and period before using the number.

A liquid wrapper around an indirect claim

Matt Levine examines the Corgi MANGOS ETF. The SEC prospectus permits private-company exposure through swaps or eligible special-purpose vehicles rather than direct share purchases. The expected swap reference uses private-company perpetual-futures prices. The ETF itself does not hold those futures or post collateral at their venue.

A swap gives contractually defined gains and losses without necessarily conveying ownership. A perpetual future has no fixed expiry. When the underlying shares are private, traders cannot reliably buy shares to arbitrage a difference between the derivative price and share value. An observable, frequently updated price can still be a poor estimate of equity value.

The counterparty has a calculation role subject to the contract and review process. Pricing, counterparty and exit risks remain. An IPO does not automatically deliver shares or convert an existing perpetual-linked swap into direct listed-share exposure. Familiar ETF packaging does not make the nested exposure familiar.

The issuer says combined OpenAI/Anthropic exposure is limited to 15% at investment and describes one-for-one, not leveraged, return exposure. That is a limit at investment, not an assertion that portfolio weights can never move. The episode explains mechanics; it is not a trading recommendation.

Texas is a discussion, not a production schedule

Asianometry explicitly has no inside information and explores possible TSMC/Terafab arrangements. It attributes to Musk: "Just discussions, but something may come of it." It says the board has not reviewed or approved the Texas fab. Bloomberg reporting, syndicated by Insurance Journal, also describes early-stage plans and policy contingencies. None establishes a committed project.

The useful question is what a foundry partnership would supply beyond capital. A chip design must meet a manufacturing process, then packaging and testing. Yield, the share of chips passing the relevant tests, changes the cost per usable chip. Installed machines are not equivalent to qualified, repeatable production.

Customer commitments, ownership and operational expertise could be split in different ways. Those are possibilities, not disclosed deal terms. For Indian semiconductor announcements, distinguish land, equipment installation, qualification, commercial output and acceptable yield. The speculative capital arithmetic in the newsletter is not treated as a forecast.

A tariff can protect one market and expose another

Mint examines JLR as the UK considers duties on Chinese EVs. The report does not say duties have been enacted. It sees limited direct overlap between Chinese EVs and JLR's premium products, while raising the risk of Chinese retaliation. That retaliation is a scenario, not a completed measure.

China retail sales fell 60% in the reported second quarter, and China's share of retail mix declined to 9% from 21%. Separately, wholesale volumes excluding the joint venture rose 25% to about 82,400. Wholesale sales into distribution and retail sales to final customers are different measures; do not merge them into a single growth rate.

Nomura forecasts a 2.6% EBIT margin versus company guidance of 4%. Both are expectations, not conflicting actual results. The policy frame is a two-sided exposure map: what the company imports, where it makes products, who competes with it and where it sells. Protection at home need not mean protection everywhere.

A new occasion still needs its own economics

United Spirits launches Smirnoff Ice in Goa, Bengaluru and Mumbai, with broader rollout planned. Mint reports 4% and 8% alcohol-by-volume variants. The ready-to-drink proposition combines brand, flavor, convenience and a finished serving. This is a business analysis, not a recommendation to drink.

Mint puts the Indian RTD category above $200m in 2025, growing about 11% annually, at around 5.3m nine-litre cases. These are category estimates, not a sales forecast for the launch. Nine-litre cases are a volume unit, not consumer package size.

Company-supplied Smirnoff net sales value was nearly Rs250cr in Q1 versus about Rs350cr for the preceding full year. That is the umbrella brand, not Ice revenue. No launch sales target is given. Brand strength may help trial; repeat purchase, retained revenue, margins and store reorders remain product-specific questions.
