The buildout is on the ballot

Every infrastructure build eventually meets its politics. For AI data centers, that meeting is tomorrow. The Information counted: in 21 of the 36 states electing a governor, at least one major-party nominee backs a moratorium on new data centers; pause candidates are poised to win in 8 states with 7 more battlegrounds, covering a third of the US population. Three sitting governors - Hochul, Abbott, Kotek - have already paused large projects. The nickname warfare (“Data Center Joe,” “Chat JKT”) and Pew’s six-in-ten discomfort number explain why: the industry lost the public before it knew there was a public. The physics, from SemiAnalysis, set the stakes: three-quarters of 2027’s 33.8 GW is already under construction, so next year is baked. The fight is 2028’s 62.2 GW, most unbuilt, most in states voting tomorrow, with Texas alone near 30% and its permits frozen since September. A moratorium is a precise weapon: it stops approvals, not construction, which is why paper projects - Stargate’s Texas, Michigan and New Mexico sites, SoftBank’s 8 GW Ohio campus - carry the risk. The industry’s response has been money (Abbott’s $9M from data-center-linked donors; both feuding AI PACs behind Florida’s Donalds) and the candidates’ answer has been conditions even where not pauses: ratepayer protections, tax-break repeal, labor and NDA rules. The era of the apolitical buildout is over.

Earnings into the inquisition

The market grades the AI trade again this week, and the setup is almost literary. Palantir reports today expecting 83% growth - a slowdown from 93% - on $22M of first-half capex, in a market punishing everyone else’s hundreds of billions. If any stock survives the capex inquisition, it is the one with no capex. Apple follows with new CEO John Ternus’s first call: three new products shipped (folding iPhone Duo, $2,899 touchscreen MacBook, $299 HomeView), early cracking-screen reports, and guided revenue growth of 9-11% with iPhone in the mid-teens, down from 22%. Uber closes Wednesday with bookings guided up 19% - and the disclosure that Instacart shopped itself to Uber before DoorDash, a deal killed by Uber’s $15B Delivery Hero buy and antitrust math. The deal of last week nearly went somewhere else entirely.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, The Briefing, Nov 1 (full text read).

The pipeline tightens

Under the earnings, the financing story turned. The Journal’s free tech letter: banks are getting choosier on AI exposure, lenders are demanding tougher terms, rates are making debt offerings less attractive, and the neoclouds are delaying or downsizing IPOs - exactly the weak link Thursday’s Nvidia-Nscale credit-guarantee story flagged. Then the tell: OpenAI, whose trillion-dollar commitments started the buildout, cut its private round from $30B to $20B at a $1.25T valuation and has raised less than half even of that. Not a death sentence - a price discovery. The strongest balance sheets are switching to debt (Amazon’s new $80B loan facility) while S&P cut Meta’s outlook to negative on AI monetization. The buildout continues. Who holds the bag is being renegotiated.

Binance survives the stress test

The FBI formally attributed the $6.3B Binance theft to North Korea on Saturday (Bloomberg, labeled). Withdrawals reopened Sunday; net outflows since are $3.6B - a run the exchange simply processed, which Bloomberg rightly calls a relatively soft blow. The freezes (Tether and Circle locking $3B at the address level) and the war room bought the one thing that matters in a bank run: credibility. Meanwhile the defense trade reprices: Cyera has discussed nearly doubling to a $20B valuation. Offense makes headlines; defense makes valuations.

Bloomberg Morning Briefing Asia, Nov 1 (newsletter text, labeled - CAPTCHA-walled). Paywalled for readers: The Information, The Briefing, Nov 1 (full text read).

Priced in orbit

Google’s data center energy chief Amanda Peterson Corio, whose company is literally testing TPUs in orbit, said space data centers cost up to 100 times terrestrial ones: “let’s explore all the terrestrial options before we go extraterrestrial.” Google’s Suncatcher prototype processed its first orbital AI workload on Friday - a haiku. The break-even is $200/kg launch costs, perhaps 2035, contingent on Starship cadence. Musk says next year; a slice of SpaceX’s $1.8T rests on it. Her other line deserves framing too: the terrestrial backlash is largely the fault of younger operators, because the companies that built for years “were doing it the right way.” One company sells the dream; another prices it.
