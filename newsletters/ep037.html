The yield does not include the exit

The Core asks whether Indian retail investors understand corporate bonds. The full newsletter, not its linked podcast, informs this segment. Its retail-flow estimate is omitted because the linked source is Economic Times-family, excluded from this brief.

A bond is a loan with payment and repayment terms. Coupon, purchase price and yield are different. Illustratively, an Rs1,000-face-value bond paying Rs80 annually has an 8% current yield at Rs1,000, 10% at Rs800 and about 6.7% at Rs1,200. Yield to maturity also considers repayment and timing. None guarantees the issuer pays.

Credit risk, interest-rate risk and liquidity risk are separate. A solvent issuer may repay in five years while a holder needs cash in five weeks. Listing does not ensure a ready buyer at a desired price. The newsletter uses Franklin Templeton's 2020 winding-up as a fund-liquidity example, not evidence every underlying bond defaulted.

SEBI maintains exchange-based lists of registered bond-platform providers. Registration concerns the intermediary, not guaranteed issuer repayment or liquidity. The August circular also distinguishes international products and tax-specific bonds. For a distribution startup, the customer experience includes disclosures, settlement, repayment and exit, not just a smooth purchase screen.

Capacity is also a balance-sheet commitment

The Wall Street Journal feature, read in full through Mint's published syndication, describes rivals becoming compute suppliers and venture firms helping startups secure clusters. Chips alone are not the service: a useful cluster needs suitable hardware, communication, power, cooling and reliability for the workload.

The report describes multiyear commitments, upfront payments sometimes around 30%, and guarantors. These are reported practices, not universal cloud terms. It cites Arcee's arrangement collapsing after a data-center delay and Cohere's warning that announced capacity can fail to arrive. Announcements and operational capacity are different milestones.

Illustratively, reserving Rs1 million monthly capacity for a year while using half can double cost per useful unit if the minimum payment remains due. Overbooking protects against shortages but creates exposure when demand misses. A Rs12 million contract with 30% upfront consumes Rs3.6 million before the remaining payment schedule. These are fictional numbers, not an actual contract quote.

Inspect minimum spending, delivery dates, delay remedies, termination and resale rights. Capital access is not enough if the obligations consume runway or the cluster arrives late. Venture-fund guarantees and reserved capacity can help, while also raising questions about incentives and risk allocation.

A fast-growing segment may still be a small segment

Mint reports AWL's preliminary Q2 FY27 update: 12% volume growth and 24% revenue growth. Food and FMCG revenue grew 33% and exceeded Rs2,000 cr. These segment figures are not the whole company's growth rate.

Edible oil represented 80% of FY26 revenue, compared with 9% food/FMCG and 11% industry essentials. Oil volume grew only 3% in the reported quarter. A smaller fast-growing business cannot instantly transform a much larger slow-growing base. Revenue weights cannot be multiplied directly by volume growth when price and mix change.

Nuvama forecasts an 8% EBITDA decline and a 2.9% margin. That is an analyst forecast, not final quarterly earnings. The prior-quarter distinction between reported and normalized EBITDA reflects commodity-derivative adjustments that require their own definition. Growth and distribution gains can be real while commodity exposure still dominates economics.

Tax the thing you want to change

The Economist newsletter identified food regulation as a topic, but its full article was bot-blocked and is not summarized here. This analysis instead uses the WHO report overview and the full Lancet Global Health study of beverage-tax implementation. These are separate sources, not substitutes presented as Economist coverage.

An ad valorem tax charges a percentage of value; a volume tax charges by quantity; a sugar-content tax depends on sugar. A fixed charge on two equal-size bottles need not reward sugar reduction. A tiered sugar charge can reward reformulation when the saving exceeds its cost and buyers accept the product.

The Lancet study discusses adoption and design, not a randomized test proving every policy has the same health effect. Buying, consuming and long-term health are different outcomes. For a consumer investor, ask about pass-through, reformulation and substitution. Policy design shapes both consumer behavior and the producer's offer. No current Indian tax schedule or personal medical recommendation is inferred.

The maturity calendar is part of the rate

WSJ's Saturday Ten-Point says Russell 2000 fell for a fifth consecutive week as bond yields rose. This is the full newsletter's observation; the linked market feature was not read. It is a brief signal, not a reconstructed market report.

Illustratively, refinancing Rs100 million from 6% to 9% adds Rs3 million annual interest before other changes. Actual sensitivity depends on maturity, fixed/floating terms, cash and pricing power. Small-company status alone does not establish fragility, and an index decline does not predict every constituent's outcome.

Higher required returns can reduce the present value of future cash flows, but earnings and sentiment change too. The portable diligence question is when debt reprices and whether cash generation makes that event manageable. A current interest bill can conceal a future dependency.
