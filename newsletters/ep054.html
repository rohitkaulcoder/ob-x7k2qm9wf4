OpenAI shops at Google’s chip store

The most valuable customer Nvidia has is planning to buy Google’s TPUs. The Information’s scoop has no financial terms, but it has the tell: a job listing for an engineer to lead optimization of OpenAI’s frontier models on TPUs, with broad technical ownership of the effort. Programs hire; experiments don’t. The history is the spicy part - when TI first reported OpenAI was using TPUs last year, Jensen Huang reportedly sprang into action and OpenAI denied having “current plans” for TPUs. The plans are now current. Same day: Oracle becomes the first outside cloud to rent TPUs, buying chips outright for large mutual customers - and OpenAI is a major Oracle customer. The backdrop is demand proof from Monday’s S-1: $114.5B committed to Google Cloud, $161.2B for TPU compute via Broadcom, $125-150B more under negotiation. TD Cowen sizes the TPU business at $9.4B in H2 sales (13% of Google Cloud) scaling to $138B by 2028 - a third of the cloud business, still a fraction of the $692B expected of Nvidia. Nvidia’s answer was to buy $3B of Meta’s upsized $97.5B stock offering while Meta increases Nvidia deployments. When both vendors purchase their customers’ equity to hold demand, the moat is the balance sheet, not the silicon. Alphabet reports today; TPUs are the first question.

Why the TPU port is hard

A GPU is a general-purpose parallel machine: thousands of flexible cores, each paying overhead for its flexibility - scheduling, branching, caches. A TPU discards all of that for a systolic array, a grid of multiply-accumulate units where data flows rhythmically and every unit does one operation and hands the result to its neighbor. Transformers are nearly all matrix multiplication, so specialization wins on efficiency per watt and per dollar. The cost is software: models must compile through Google’s XLA toolchain onto a fixed execution plan, and anything that doesn’t map to the array falls off the fast path. That is what the OpenAI job listing is actually for. And it reframes Monday’s 85% gross margin: Anthropic built Borg-inspired abstractions that let one workload land on Nvidia, Google, Amazon or AMD silicon and pick the best chip per job - co-evolving Amazon’s Trainium along the way. Once your workloads are portable, every chip vendor becomes a bidder. OpenAI is now hiring to build the same leverage.

The $63 billion first slice

Monday we quoted the $161 billion Broadcom line; Tuesday brought the mechanism for its first slice. Per the Journal’s AI newsletter: Broadcom and Google design the chips, TSMC builds them, a bank-funded SPV buys them against Broadcom guarantees, and leases them to Anthropic, whose payments service the debt. Banks only moved the $63 billion of paper after Broadcom sweetened guarantees and took higher rates. Zoom out: big tech sells ~$250B of bonds this year and ~$400B next; CoreWeave is down 35% in a month; Meta’s long-term debt went from under $20B to over $80B, and its equity raise starts with a $37.5B step anchored by Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom. The Journal’s frame is right: Anthropic’s rivals are rooting for this IPO because a $100B reception re-rates everyone’s cost of capital - OpenAI needs it for its own listing next year. Ciena’s CEO, a dot-com optical survivor, is “suitably paranoid.” Circular is no longer an insult; it is the architecture.

Price is not the lever

OpenAI’s answer to Anthropic’s lead has been to become the budget option. It isn’t working. Its cheapest model undercuts Anthropic’s Haiku by 80% on Artificial Analysis tasks and won Rippling as a switcher, but Opus 5.5’s release swung OpenRouter traffic back: capability still beats price. Meanwhile Anthropic posts 900% net revenue retention, twelve-fold growth in $100k+ enterprises to ~11,900, roughly 40% more revenue than OpenAI, and a filing pledge to keep Claude ad-free - while locking enterprises into non-cancellable multi-year contracts to match its $657B of compute commitments. The market context: Anthropic is under 1% of its largest Fortune 500 customers’ expenses and under 0.1% for the rest. The whole market is a rounding error in the labor budget. That is either the bull case or the bubble case. OpenAI’s product response, GPT-6.1 Terra, shipped Monday - cheaper and more efficient, which is the answer to a question customers aren’t asking first.

Mars merger pay

Matt Levine asked the best question of the SpaceX-Tesla merger chatter: what happens to the two pay packages? Tesla’s - 424 million shares across 12 tranches, gated on market caps from $2T to $8.5T plus operational milestones - dies in any change of control below $2 trillion, because only already-earned shares vest at the merger value and the rest is forfeited. Tesla trades at ~$1.5 trillion. The package evaporates on contact. SpaceX’s - 1.3 billion shares - pays nothing unless the company establishes a million-person Mars colony or builds 100 terawatts of non-Earth data centers; those milestones, Levine notes, do not adjust for acquisitions, so a Mars-colony acquisition technically checks the box. Scale the market-cap milestones for a Tesla merger the way the xAI deal scaled them (~25%) and Musk needs a ~$13 trillion combined company, plus Mars or the space data centers, to collect - with about 78% of the votes, since SpaceX has super-voting stock and Tesla doesn’t. His conclusion: the package would be worth a trillion, his existing stake four, so it’s not about the money. It’s never about the money. Tesla shareholders are being asked to hand their company to someone who just lost a trillion dollars of incentive to grow it.

Money Stuff, Oct 27 (free, full read).

Anduril’s slope, Spark’s windfall

Anduril hopes to raise early next year at around $120 billion - double May’s $61B round, above the $90B employee share sale, above the $100B Reuters floated in July. The slope is the story; the justification is contract momentum (an Army Lattice deal that could reach $1.8B) against a revenue forecast of $4.3B, a sliver of Lockheed’s $80B. Hardware priced on software hopes. Meanwhile the Anthropic cap-table math landed: Spark Capital accumulated 12.2 million shares across six rounds for $777M - over half in May’s Series H - worth $11B+ at a $1.5T debut. A $9B win on under $1B for a 21-year-old generalist fund; Yasmin Razavi remains the only VC on Anthropic’s board; Spark is raising a $3B fund off the momentum. This is the return that makes a franchise, and every LP deck in venture is about to feature it.
