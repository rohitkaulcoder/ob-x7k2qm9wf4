The web becomes bits perfect

Agent traffic is already approaching human traffic by one estimate cited in Sajith Pai's newsletter. The exact forecast matters less than the change in customer journey.

The human web turns intent into revenue through navigation, product pages and checkout. An agent needs live inventory, clear prices, stable APIs, permissions and evidence that an action succeeded. Pixels become schemas. Persuasion becomes execution.

This does not remove the human. The human owns the goal and money. The agent becomes the decision-and-transaction proxy. Serve the proxy badly and the human disappears without visiting.

The new shelf is inside the model

Agent commerce needs boring controls. Idempotency stops retries from buying twice. Receipts prove success. Permission manifests define what a tool may do. Reversible operations keep a bad interpretation from becoming permanent.

Interface power does not disappear. It moves. Agents inherit biases from training and tool access. Some already favour familiar infrastructure providers. The next optimisation industry will target model recommendations as search optimisation targeted result pages.

The dark pattern is invisible conversion. Synthetic reviews, selective benchmarks and merchant commissions can shape a decision inside a context window the buyer never sees. Who owns the shortlist becomes the app-store question of the agent era.

Nua's return came from the hard part

Nua's $50M Series C gave Kae Capital a partial exit at 18 times invested capital on the sold part of its Fund II position. Kae wrote the first institutional cheque, Rs 5 crore, nearly eight years ago.

Menstrual care looked hostile: strong incumbents, offline distribution and a high cost of product failure. Nua built online trust and repeat use first. Modern trade followed. Quick commerce accelerated discovery.

The sequence matters. Distribution amplified a product that already worked. It did not rescue one that did not. Kae sold enough to improve DPI and retained upside. Paper marks tell a story. Cash distributions let limited partners believe it.

Local AI needs a learning loop

Kae sees a right to win for Indian consumer-AI founders in vernacular communication, wellness and Bharat interfaces. Being Indian is not a moat. Learning from Indian usage can be.

A frontier lab supplies intelligence. It does not automatically own local trust, fragmented supply, payment behaviour or language context. Each interaction must make the local system better.

Localisation is translation. A local learning architecture is compounding data plus execution. Tough markets produce asymmetric returns only when usage intensifies. Without repeat, they remain merely tough.

Cheap models move value into the physical world

One framework in Sajith Pai's edition says American labs treat the model as the product while Chinese firms may commoditise it to capture value in energy, robotics and production.

Shein is the useful example. It senses trends, makes designs, orders small factory batches, observes demand and changes production. The valuable asset is the coupling between knowledge and physical execution.

If models become cheap, India need not own the largest one to capture value. It can own the workflow that closes a loop in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare or finance. The question is not how smart the model is. It is which real system improves every time it acts.

AI safety enters the culture war

Dario Amodei and effective altruism have become recurring New York Post targets just as Anthropic calls for a frontier slowdown and outside model evaluation. A survey cited by The Information says 63% of American adults think AI could eventually destroy the world. Catastrophe is plausible. The proposed experts are suspect.

This is an institutional problem. Safety rules framed as a few labs and one ideology choosing the future will be brittle, especially when those rules can entrench incumbents.

Credibility cannot rest on sincerity. It requires transparent tests, plural oversight, published failures and rules that constrain Anthropic as sharply as competitors. Less apocalypse theatre. More boring institutions with the authority to inspect and report.

Source: The Information Weekend, Sep 19, 2026 · subscriber edition

Access can masquerade as product-market fit

Advent is buying 24.9% of Yatharth Hospitals for Rs 3,150 crore. L Catterton is backing Bengaluru restaurant group Nandhana Foods. Global capital is buying scaled regional systems where operational improvement can compound.

Defense tech presents the darker version. An investor quoted in a book highlighted by The Information says hypersonic-weapon returns depend more on government relationships and lobbying than on the product.

In a monopsony, the dominant buyer defines the market. Distribution is part of product-market fit. It is not proof of product value. The investor's question is whether access creates a feedback loop that improves the product or protects it from comparison.
