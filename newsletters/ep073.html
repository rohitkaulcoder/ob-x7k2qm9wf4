The week the AI trade gets repriced

Two events, each of which would ordinarily own a month of news, land within days of each other. Tomorrow Nvidia reports its fiscal third quarter, the first earnings since Jensen Huang resigned, with Colette Kress making her debut as interim chief executive on the call. And this week Anthropic is expected to price the largest initial public offering in history. The numbers are genuinely absurd. Anthropic’s preliminary range is $70 to $78 a share, a valuation of $1.26 to $1.4 trillion, and The Information reports demand is intense enough to price above it: $84 or $85 gets you to $1.5 trillion, with the desk’s bet that the stock opens well above $100, possibly above $150. The book was already reported six times covered last week. For scale: to pass Apple, the largest market cap in the world at about $5.2 trillion, Anthropic shares would have to clear $288. Peers is asking how fast it gets there, not whether. The revenue math, per The Information’s Dealmaker: Anthropic did $17 billion in the third quarter against OpenAI’s $10 billion, and was generating $81 billion annualized as of October. At $1.5 trillion that is roughly nineteen times sales, fifteen times if you believe the $100 billion it exits the year at. Either every private foundation-model round gets marked up this morning, or the public market is about to teach private markets a lesson. For an India fund, every AI application company in the portfolio just got a new comparable, and it is a wild one.

The demand question

Nvidia’s print itself will be spectacular: projected revenue growth around 89%, a reacceleration from last year’s “slowdown” of 66%. Nobody is tuning in for the numbers. The stock closed Friday at $162.85, down 13% on the week and 32% since early October, and HSBC downgraded to a hold Friday citing long-term uncertainty. The real question is what the revenue is made of: how much growth came from chips leaking into China through channels like Megaspeed, now being shut down, and how much demand was juiced by Nvidia’s own investments in the neoclouds that buy its chips. Meanwhile the state has moved in. Treasury Secretary Bessent says Nvidia needs a “dramatic shakeup” in leadership, an extraordinary public instruction from a cabinet secretary to a company of this size. The Financial Times reports Huang is discussing a comeback with Silicon Valley allies lobbying for his return. A House chairman says Huang has agreed to testify, no date set, and Bloomberg reports Nvidia quietly sought Taipei’s help over an employee’s chip-smuggling case. Three things to listen for on the call: any disclosure on China revenue now that enforcement bites, whether Kress touches the circularity question at all, and her tone. A caretaker reading a lawyer’s script keeps the pressure on; an operator with a plan starts the comeback trade. Trump meets Xi and Putin in Shenzhen this week, and chip smuggling is now a head-of-state agenda item.

Potatoes and power

Ulanqab, in Inner Mongolia, used to be famous for potatoes. Now it may be where the future of AI data centers got plugged in first. Envision Energy, a Shanghai-based renewables company, is running an 800-volt direct-current power system at its Galaxy data center there, the first live deployment it claims anywhere; Nvidia and its partners are still preparing the equivalent for next year’s hardware. Why voltage matters, from first principles: power equals voltage times current, and current is the enemy, because cable losses scale with the square of the current and high current demands thick, expensive copper. Meanwhile grid power arrives as alternating current and chips eat direct current, so electricity is converted back and forth several times before it reaches a server and every conversion wastes a few percent. Envision’s solid-state transformers take 10,000-volt AC straight off the grid and output 800-volt DC to the racks at 98.5% efficiency, versus the typical 94 to 95. On a gigawatt campus, four points of efficiency is tens of megawatts you no longer have to buy. And the stakes are compounding: Blackwell racks draw around 140 kilowatts, next year’s Vera Rubin racks up to 227, and the Kyber racks arriving 2027 about 600 kilowatts per rack.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, AI Infrastructure (“How a Chinese Energy Company Is Outpacing the U.S. on AI Power Technology”), Nov 15 (full text in email).

Constraint bred capability

The reason China moved first is the chip war itself. Export rules keep Nvidia’s best silicon out, so Chinese racks lash together more, weaker chips: ByteDance showed a design in July needing up to 500 kilowatts, more than double anything Nvidia sells today. Chinese operators hit the power wall earlier and had to solve it earlier. Envision’s own executives expect two power architectures to emerge, one following Nvidia’s international standard and one for China, mirroring the split in chips, and the company is already pitching pilots in Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific. The skeptic’s footnotes: this is a five-megawatt pilot, the system costs 10 to 15 percent more up front, operators will not risk downtime on billions of GPUs for four points of conversion loss until the technology is proven, and Envision itself expects about two years to broad adoption. But the direction is set. Power is the real bottleneck of the buildout, not chips and not models, and the picks-and-shovels layer of transformers, switchgear and cooling is wide open, including in India’s own power-constrained data-center buildout.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, AI Infrastructure, Nov 15 (full text in email).

The shadow banks get institutional

India’s non-bank lenders pulled in nearly a billion dollars of private equity in a single week. Blackstone is leading $686 million into IIFL Finance, $535 million of it Blackstone’s own money at Rs 602 a share for about a 16% stake, with promoters Nirmal Jain and R Venkataraman and Anchorage Capital filling out the round. Temasek is putting $265 million into Godrej Capital, the Godrej group’s five-year-old financial services arm, at just shy of a $3 billion valuation; the company targets one lakh crore of assets by 2031, up from Rs 35,000 crore today. The EY-IVCA trendbook says financial services was India’s single biggest PE-VC sector this calendar year, $11.9 billion across 227 deals, a fifth of all private capital deployed. The thesis is the gap between formal banks and hundreds of millions of underserved borrowers. And the regulator noticed the same week the money did: Reuters reports the RBI is drafting rules to stop PE firms holding controlling stakes in more than one non-bank lender, with a 15% threshold separating financial investment from ownership, formal rules expected in 2027. Read Blackstone’s 16% against that line: the smart money is pricing the rulebook, not guessing at it. India has the scar tissue for shadow-banking blowups; IL&FS was 2018. A central bank moving before the excess, not after, is what institutional maturity looks like. People and funds: Anup Gupta is stepping down from Nexus Venture Partners after sixteen years and a foundational run including Turtlemint, India Shelter and MakeMyTrip, and Norwest closed its eighteenth global fund at $4 billion, its largest ever.

The Runway (Snigdha), Edition #54, Nov 15 (full text in email); RBI proposals per Reuters as carried in The Runway.

Right beats first

The most interesting consumer AI story of the year finally got told from the inside: Lenny Rachitsky’s first interview with Mona Sarantakos, the VP of Product running Meta’s Muse, the number one app in the App Store and arguably the fastest-growing consumer AI product ever. The origin is a weekend hack with OpenClaw on a Raspberry Pi. Then the team waited seven months to launch, despite starting before most of its competitors, while Nat Friedman, Alex Wang and Zuckerberg himself each left fingerprints on it. In an industry screaming about shipping velocity, the biggest consumer AI hit of the year sat in the oven. The team runs “agent swarms” to surface user insights at scale, a placeholder avatar named Jolly became an icon, and Sarantakos says exactly two levers actually move activation for an AI agent. The skeptic’s note: number one in the App Store is a marketing metric when you own the largest distribution funnel on earth; retention is the number that matters and nobody outside Meta has it. Still, the delay is the signal: Meta concluded that in consumer AI, being right beats being first. At Apple, Gurman reports the just-announced Siri AI app integrations are the centerpiece of the smart-glasses push, the glasses pitch being Siri acting inside your apps rather than a screen on your face; the first iOS 27.3 beta finally ships the precise app-control Siri demoed two and a half years ago, and new CEO John Ternus is personally pressing Apple’s software chief to get more serious about AI.

Lenny’s Newsletter (Mona Sarantakos interview show notes), Nov 15. Paywalled for readers: Bloomberg, Power On (Mark Gurman), Nov 15 (newsletter text).

The other big AI story

While the market stared at the Huang scandal, the research nonprofit METR released a report on November 5 finding that Anthropic’s pace of capability improvement is accelerating, and that most of the acceleration comes from Anthropic using its own AI to do AI research. The machines are now meaningfully building the next machines. Two readings, both interpretation rather than established fact. The bull reading: the early curve of recursive self-improvement, with Anthropic’s lead compounding from here, which happens to be exactly the story a company wants in the market the week it prices an IPO at a trillion and a half, so discount accordingly. The cautious reading: METR measures benchmark-style tasks, benchmarks saturate, and acceleration on a task suite is not acceleration toward general intelligence. Both can be partly true. What is not interpretation: anyone pricing AI companies this quarter now has to decide whether capability curves are linear or compounding, and the answer is worth about a trillion dollars.

Bloomberg, The Forecast (Weekend), Nov 15 (newsletter text); METR report, Nov 5, as described therein.

Quick hits

SpaceX caught the Starship upper stage with the launch tower’s arms Saturday, a first; the ship was the missing half of full reusability, though this flight’s booster ended up in the Gulf, so one launch recovering both is still ahead of them. OpenAI announced a ChatGPT digital wallet Friday: its Dot assistant can buy things online without touching your card details, which is OpenAI coming for the checkout layer. The White House AI task force held its second meeting Thursday, working on a voluntary framework for testing frontier models and hardening critical infrastructure. The Information scoops that Musk’s xAI has gutted the Grok video-generation team. Venezuelan crypto app Kontigo had its license suspended, cutting it off from the country’s banks. In Indian consumer deals, Bodhi Tree, the James Murdoch and Uday Shankar vehicle, is buying a majority stake in Adyar Ananda Bhavan, the fifty-year-old Chennai restaurant chain, for $180 million, and Warburg Pincus is taking control of Pricol Autotech in a $176.5 million deal with a public listing to follow. Weekend long-read: Asianometry’s history of the Ural series, the workhorse computer the Soviet Union built after America refused to sell it a “robot calculator” in 1946. Eighty years later the US is restricting chip exports again, and China is building workhorse computers of its own. Some rhymes take generations.
