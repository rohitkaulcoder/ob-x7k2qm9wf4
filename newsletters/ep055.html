Two Alphabets, one bill

Earnings night handed us the cleanest split screen of the AI cycle so far. On one side: Google Cloud more than doubled year over year to north of $30B, and operating income tripled to $11.12B - within striking distance of Azure’s $33B (up 48%). The growth engine is exactly the one tracked here all week: enterprise AI workloads, the TPU business, and the big labs’ compute commitments converting into booked revenue. On the other side: Alphabet fell 10% after hours, because search advertising grew just 12%, the softest since Q2 2025, even as AI Mode drove the highest query growth in five years. People are searching more than ever; the answers just monetize worse than ten blue links, and management declined to quantify the gap. Beneath both: free cash flow of negative $9B, against positive $24B a year ago. There are now two Alphabets - an infrastructure company growing faster than ever, and an advertising company whose own product dilutes its monetization - and one capex bill for both.

The metric Microsoft stopped telling you

Microsoft’s mirror image came with a fascinating omission. Shares rose 3% after hours and Microsoft 365 Commercial Cloud grew 17% to $27.7B - but the Copilot paying-seat metric vanished from disclosure. The last number ever given was 30M seats in June; in its place, “usage-paying customers up 75%” and CFO Amy Hood pointing to ARPU. Translation: when a company retires a metric, the metric stopped helping the story. Mechanically, this is the shift from charging per human seat to charging per agent action, which decouples AI revenue from daily active humans - and makes adoption far harder to model. Thirty million seats was already modest against the Office installed base; the silence suggests the curve flattened. Watch every other vendor copy this disclosure change. Oracle rounded out the night, up 10% on a guide that capex peaks in FY2028-29, free cash flow turns positive in FY2028 and exceeds $30B by FY2029 - a three-year promise to survive on faith and backlog, and that backlog remains the industry’s most concentrated bet on a single AI customer.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, The Briefing, Oct 28 (full text read).

The security bill comes due

The follow-up to this month’s vulnerability wave: the spending spree has begun. The Information’s “Nobody’s Ready” stacks up the anecdotes - Atomicwork’s CEO dropping everything when a root CA, a popular LLM library and PHP all broke at once; Bank of America hiring 1,000 contractors for AI-assisted patching with Anthropic’s Mythos; Goldman Sachs running Mythos and OpenAI’s Astra for bug hunts, with one insider saying AI-security spend may top AI-coding spend; an energy provider raising a $30M budget 10% and pulling next year’s forward; Palo Alto Networks’ growth doubling past 30%. The number that ends board meetings: twelve firms with early Mythos access found 37,000+ open-source vulnerabilities in a month, against a global baseline of roughly 1,500. Epoch AI adds that August and September alone produced more high-severity vulnerabilities than all of 2025. The disclosure machinery of the software industry - CVE databases, triage teams, patch cadences - was built for a trickle of human-found bugs, not a firehose. Every dollar of AI productivity is arriving with an involuntary matching dollar of remediation.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, Nobody’s Ready for the AI Bug Bounty Boom, Oct 28 (full text read).

Amazon builds the agent mall it won’t let anyone else into

Shop Direct now carries 250M listings from outside retailers, 2.5x since March - while Amazon has spent over a year blocking outside shopping agents from its site and quietly built its own cross-retail agent, Buy for Me, on Anthropic’s models. Merchants are confused: enrollment was opt-out, some listings link to unauthorized resellers, and sales are scarce. Why it matters: retail media is one of Amazon’s highest-margin businesses, and an agent that skips the search page skips the ads. Whoever owns the agent owns the shelf placement of the future. Note the sequence - block others first, build your own second, open the gates third. Jassy’s line about welcoming outside agents “if they improve and offer attractive economics” is a take-rate negotiation in waiting. Meanwhile ChatGPT’s in-chat checkout was scrapped in March and Meta’s Muse orders are surfacing at Logicbroker clients. Combined with this week’s Stripe agent-payments launch, the rails and the storefront of agentic commerce are being rebuilt at the same time, by incumbents who intend to own both ends.

Distillation rotates to OpenAI

DeepSeek and Moonshot AI both distilled OpenAI’s newest model, GPT-6 Astra, for their latest releases - DeepSeek’s V4.1-Flash and Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro - per The Information. Distillation is capability cloning on the cheap: ask the teacher millions of questions, harvest the answers, train the student on the pairs. Anthropic used to be the top target until its crackdown cut harvested data 90-99%, so the distillers rotated. Anthropic’s S-1 now prices the traffic: terms-violating customers were 5-7% of revenue, which against $33B+ through September implies roughly $1.7-2.3B a year (illustratively), with one proxy operator spending about $10M a month. OpenAI’s annualized revenue neared $50B, up 70% since June - an even juicier teacher. DeepSeek’s Liang Wenfeng reportedly resisted distillation on principle, then relented after Z.ai’s GLM climbed the leaderboards. If a $100M training run can be approximated for a few million in API bills, enforcement - not architecture - becomes the moat, and Anthropic is walking into history’s biggest IPO with a risk factor its competitors call a subsidy.

Paywalled for readers: The Information, Chinese Labs Distill OpenAI’s Latest Model, Oct 28 (full text read).

The 20-month gap

Nikunj Kothari’s essay in The Bridge, backed by Carta data: the median seed-to-Series-A gap now runs past 20 months, and the bottleneck is narrative, not product. Founders pitch what they’ve built; the A asks how the machine scales. “At seed we bet on spiky founders with unique insight; at the A we bet you can scale the magic.” The spike that wins the seed is not the systems thinking that wins the A - and for Indian founders, where the A market is thinner, a 20-month median is a runway-planning problem: raise for 24, and rehearse the scaling story six months early. Alongside: Hex Technologies raised $100M at a $1.2B valuation led by Peak XV, and Live2.ai raised ₹130 crore (~$14M) in a pre-Series B led by IvyCap.
