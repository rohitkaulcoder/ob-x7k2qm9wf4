The advertised price is not the workload price

Clouded Judgement argues that cache is the new egress: model customers should care about the cost of repeated use, not merely their first request. Prompt caching reuses intermediate computation for matching input, rather than returning a saved answer irrespective of the new question. OpenAI's public documentation makes the exact-prefix requirement clear. Routing, retention and availability determine whether a hit actually occurs.

Illustrative arithmetic, not provider pricing: ten requests each have 10,000 input tokens, of which 8,000 form a stable prefix. At $10 per million ordinary tokens and $1 per million cached tokens, no caching costs $1. With one initial ordinary request and nine perfect reuses, ordinary input costs $0.28and cached input $0.072: total $0.352. This excludes output and assumes no separate write premium. Actual model families may charge for writes.

Ball's stronger thesis is that long-running applications can face switching costs when reusable state must be rebuilt. That is plausible friction, not proof of permanent lock-in. The original documents and a provider's finite-lived computed cache are different things. Compare cost per successfully completed job, including failures, retries, output and latency. A low token bill can still buy expensive work.

The IPO needs a cash-flow story, not only a software story

Mint reports Zetwerk is seeking an IPO valuation around Rs27,000 cr ($3.1 bn), with more than30investors considering the anchor book. This is reported interest, not confirmed subscriptions or a final price. The expected structure is Rs2,830 cr of fresh issuance plus Rs2,200 cr of sales by existing holders. The combined Rs5,030 cr is not all new operating capital.

Zetwerk coordinates manufacturing through software, its own facilities and third-party suppliers. Software can improve coordination without making every rupee of underlying manufacturing revenue high-margin software revenue. Public investors will need the offering documents to understand delivery obligations, supplier risk, margins and working capital.

The article cites estimated FY26operating revenue of Rs15,900 cr, up 24%, via earlier reporting tied to a rating rationale, and a prior $4 bn funding valuation. These are differently sourced figures and differently structured transactions, not a complete return calculation. The report says a price-band announcement is expected Oct 12while also discussing an RHP filing within two weeks. That timing is unresolved; we do not invent a subscription calendar.

A green label cannot fund a factory

The Core reports that a proposed Rs15,000 cr green-steel mission has narrowed to a Rs5,000 cr scheme still awaiting approval. This is Friday daytime catch-up, not a new cabinet-status check. It argues that large producers can invest while smaller mills lack the capital to follow.

Steel emissions arise from both processing chemistry and energy. Electric does not mean zero-carbon: electricity, feedstock and the measured boundary matter. India's official taxonomy defines green-rated steel below 2.2 tonnes CO2-equivalent per tonne of finished steel, with five-star below 1.6. Green-rated is a benchmark-relative description, not zero emissions. Crude-steel and finished-steel figures cannot be compared as exact equivalents.

The European Commission says CBAM's definitive phase applies to 2026 imports of covered goods, including selected iron and steel. Authorized importers declare embedded emissions and surrender certificates, with rules covering thresholds and carbon prices already paid. Certificate purchases start February 2027. The liability period and payment timetable differ; this is not a cash tax collected on every shipment today.

A lower-emissions product can be more attractive to a buyer facing carbon-related costs. But regulation does not automatically make the technology affordable for the supplier. Financing, measured performance and credible purchase commitments have to meet. A taxonomy can coordinate demand; it cannot substitute for capital or an offtake contract.

Forty is a score, not a business model

a16z's weekly charts argues that the Rule of Forty has weakened as a valuation signal. It adds revenue growth to a profitability margin. Thirty-percent growth plus ten-percent margin and ten-percent growth plus thirty-percent margin both score 40. Those illustrative companies need not deserve the same valuation.

The newsletter interprets improved combined scores alongside lower multiples as a shift from growth to margin, while markets still pay more for perceived growth. That is an interpretation of charts, not a universal causal finding. The separate political prediction-market section is not part of this episode.

For SaaS diligence, retain the components. A margin improvement may reflect pricing power, better economics or less investment in future demand. A low margin may reflect valuable investment or weak economics. Inspect retention, expansion, sales efficiency and the forecast, rather than promoting the score into an answer. a16z is an investor and discloses an investment announcement in this issue.

A relief rally does not sign a customer contract

Mint reports Nifty IT rose 2.3% Friday as lower-than-indicated OpenAI revenue eased part of the disruption narrative. That is an attributed market interpretation, not proof one headline caused every trade. Yesterday's revenue correction is not repeated as a new standalone story.

TCS's Q2FY27sequential constant-currency revenue growth was 0.5%; international business grew 1.2%. Deal total contract value was $9.6 bn, up 1.1% sequentially but down 4% on-year, excluding named Porsche and Best Buy deals. Contract value is not all current-quarter revenue or cash collected. Demand commentary remained cautious.

Mint describes margin pressure from AI investment and subcontracting, and a possible 50 bp acquisition-consolidation effect depending on MHP timing. That is a contingent expectation, not an actual booked loss. The 26-28% long-term margin aspiration has no promised date. AI can create implementation work while changing billing economics. Productivity gains accrue through competition and contract terms, not automatically to one party.
