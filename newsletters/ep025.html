Sparse doesn't mean small

GLM-5.3 uses sparse attention to consult fewer past tokens when producing the next one. This lowers some arithmetic and memory traffic. It does not automatically shrink the memory needed to hold the past. The index that selects the most relevant tokens may still need the whole context in HBM, the fast memory beside a GPU. That distinction matters more than the word "sparse."

During inference a model stores keys and values representing previous tokens, the KV cache. Dense attention consults a broad set of these notes. Sparse attention selects the top K. If only 10,000 out of a million notes are read in the main attention operation, bandwidth use falls. But the full filing cabinet may still need to be nearby to decide which notes to read. Capacity and bandwidth are separate constraints.

SGLang's HiSparse moves KV entries between GPU HBM and larger, slower host DRAM. A least-recently-used policy keeps a hot set near the GPU and evicts older entries; on a miss it fetches one back. It tries to overlap the fetch for one layer with computation in the preceding layer. If the transfer finishes in time, the delay is hidden. If it does not, the user waits. Long-running agents still need their history somewhere. Moving it down the memory hierarchy may shift spending toward DRAM, not erase it.

The benchmark warning is sharper. SemiAnalysis compares modeled serving costs at a 150-token-per-second streaming target. One low-cost GB200 configuration had a p90 time to first token of 14-19 seconds; the compared AMD MI355X setup was around 1.3-1.7 seconds. Add a requirement of first token under two seconds and use only tested configurations: a B200 result is still about 47% cheaper per million total tokens than the AMD setup, not the 82% gap in the looser comparison. Serving software and constraints differ, so this is not a pure chip benchmark. Users notice the first pause. Investors should too.

Contract cliffs in the model price war

Anthropic and OpenAI both discount usage for large enterprise commitments. The Information reports a crucial difference at the cap: buyers from three software firms say Anthropic can end the discount immediately when committed tokens run out, pushing the customer to renegotiate or pay list. People close to OpenAI say its buyers get the remainder of the month plus another month before a return to list pricing. These are reported contract practices, not universal terms for every customer.

Some Anthropic discounts run around 15%. If a buyer goes $2 million beyond its commitment and the discount disappears, the difference against keeping a 15% reduction is $300,000. The cap is a procurement event, not merely a usage metric. Buyers should negotiate alerts, overage pricing and a right to true-up before a successful product creates a penalty.

OpenAI is cutting prices to win spend. CodeRabbit's CEO says it shifted from a mostly Anthropic model mix six months ago to OpenAI as its main supplier, with quality and pricing involved. Anthropic reportedly had more than 100 firms spending above $10 million each over the year ending June. Both labs seek "share of wallet" language, though advisers say it is difficult to verify. The model may be replaceable technically and sticky contractually. An Indian application vendor needs a fallback model and a benchmark of cost per completed task at a defined latency, not a screenshot of the cheapest token quote.

The debt hidden in the buildout

The Information's AI Infrastructure newsletter cites Moody's estimate of $2.8 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments by Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Oracle as of June 30. These include future leases, purchase agreements and guarantees. "Off balance sheet" does not mean no one owes anything. It means a conventional debt number may miss future contractual cash and contingent support.

A developer needs land, power, cooling, chips and a tenant before a centre earns rent. A long lease can support borrowing. Delay power or construction, and the lender must work out which party pays during the gap. A chip supplier's support or a cloud tenant's guarantee can move risk without eliminating it. Credit analysis needs the whole chain, not just one company's bonds.

The scale remains forecast, not funded. A Brookings paper cited in the newsletter projects $10.3 trillion of AI infrastructure investment from 2025 to 2032; a developer projects $15 trillion over a shorter span. Morgan Stanley estimates $1.4 trillion in next-year AI capex for six large companies; HSBC counts about $500 billion of investment-grade AI-related debt issued overall. These measures overlap and use different definitions. Do not add them to manufacture a single funding gap. The investor question is which sites have permits, electricity, customers and capital at terms that survive a delay.

Source: The Information, AI Infrastructure newsletter, Sep 28, 2026 (subscriber newsletter)

The agent watches the agent

Sierra has expanded Ghostwriter, its tool for building customer-facing agents. Upstarts reports a version tested with about 40 customers that studies interactions, proposes fixes and can simulate changes before a human approves them. In one example it flagged a broken API after a customer's update. The pitch is a loop: observe, suggest, test, approve, release, measure. Customer interaction data could be a durable advantage over a rented model.

That loop can also repeat mistakes quickly. Reading conversations and proposing production changes require narrow permissions, tests and rollback. "Self-improving" is not a safety property. Meanwhile Meta hired MongoDB CEO CJ Desai to lead an enterprise platform, The Information says. Meta already sells ads to many businesses but failed with Workplace. A marketing relationship does not make an IT procurement relationship. Enterprise trust and data access are separate products.

Sources: Upstarts, Sep 28, 2026; The Information, The Briefing, Sep 28, 2026 (subscriber newsletter)

Capital has a price in India too

Bloomberg's dated Asia market snapshot put the US 10-year Treasury yield at 5.24% and Brent futures at about $105 as US-Iran talks failed to ease inflation fears. These are observations at the newsletter's timestamp, not live quotes. Higher benchmark yields reprice new data-centre debt; expensive crude can pressure India's import bill, currency and logistics margins. An AI facility with a cheap-power presentation still has to borrow, import servers and connect to a grid.

Vinod Khosla told The Information he expects more than half of robotics startups to be worth less than their current valuations by 2030, alongside a few large winners. That is one investor's forecast, and his firm has robotics investments. The broader principle applies to both robots and AI infrastructure: a real technical advance does not excuse paying any price for capital.
