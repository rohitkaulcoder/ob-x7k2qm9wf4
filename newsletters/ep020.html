Ep 20: The Report Card for GPU Landlords

Overnight Brief, written edition · Thu, Sep 24, 2026 · audio: 15:37
🎧 Listen to the audio episode

You rent the rowers, not the oars

SemiAnalysis has published ClusterMAX 3.0, its rating of GPU clouds. It tested 77 providers and tracks 323. CoreWeave and Nebius are Platinum. Google joins Oracle in Gold. Azure moves to Silver. Crusoe falls to the bottom of Bronze.

An H100 is an H100, so why grade the landlord? Because a lab does not rent a chip. It rents a cluster: thousands of GPUs that must act as one computer. Training stops and syncs thousands of times an hour. One sick GPU or flaky cable can freeze the whole job and send it back to the last checkpoint. It is a rowing crew. The boat goes as fast as nobody falls out.

The metric that matters is goodput, useful work after crashes and restarts. Take a thousand GPUs at $2 an hour, about $48,000 a day. Lose three useful hours a day to failures and rollbacks and you burn about $6,000 a day, roughly $180,000 a month. A reliable cloud at $2.20 is cheaper than an unreliable one at $2. SemiAnalysis puts it simply: cost comes down to goodput, and goodput comes down to reliability.

So the product is not the chip. It is the health checks, the spare nodes, the up-to-date software and the speed of repair. That is the unglamorous part, and it is why Nebius can charge a premium.

Bronze grade, $30.9B valuation

Crusoe just raised $3.9B at a $30.9B valuation. Newcomer says it is on track for about $2B of revenue this year. It signed a $65M-a-year deal to run Thinking Machines Lab's models, and its managed inference business has more than $100M of contracted annual revenue.

SemiAnalysis, meanwhile, saw more real hardware errors on one Crusoe cluster in five days than in the rest of its testing combined, plus the oldest kernel and GPU drivers it tested. Crusoe says it has fixed the old versions.

Both can be true because Crusoe is three businesses. A campus builder with, per SemiAnalysis, the largest real pipeline in the industry. A respected inference service. And a rented-cluster cloud that is flaky. Investors are paying for the first two. The report card grades the third.

The same report explains the financing. A contract with a creditworthy customer lets a neocloud borrow below its own credit. Nvidia now supplies revenue floors and guarantees that help neoclouds reach investment-grade debt. "The balance sheet is the moat." For operators, reliability is the product. For lenders, the contract is the product. These are graded by different people.

The money stopped. The diligence did not.

Three Founders Fund partners toured companies in Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen last month, including MiniMax and UBTech. Khosla partners have gone two years running. Dimension and Thrive have been too. None of it produced an investment.

US VC deals into China are about 105 this year, down 80% from 533 in 2021, and outbound investment rules on AI and chips took effect in 2025. The trips are about something else: how dependent US portfolio companies are on Chinese supply chains, and whether a Chinese rival already makes the same product cheaper.

The facts justify the airfare. Unitree and AgiBot shipped 71% of the world's humanoid robots last year. China holds 63% of the key humanoid component suppliers. Carnegie China says China's share of top AI researchers rose from 27.1% in 2022 to 40.6% in 2025, while the US share fell from 46.4% to 34.2%.

For Indian deep-tech investing, every hardware or open-model startup has a Chinese comparable and often a Chinese supplier. Underwriting without knowing Shenzhen is half the diligence.

Sources: The Information, Sep 23, 2026 (subscriber); The Information, The Briefing, Sep 23, 2026

A discount that raises the bill

Microsoft has told sales staff they can offer 30-50% off Copilot for customers who commit to many seats and extra usage payments. Amazon is giving its merchants a free year of Quick Plus. Meanwhile, Apollo's Torsten Slok notes that credit investors in hyperscaler bonds assume operating cash flow triples between 2025 and 2030.

Discounting the seat is not generosity. More seats mean more metered usage. The customer pays less per person and more in total. Customers will work this out.

The bond market is pricing a tripling of cash flow while the sales floor offers half off. One of those will give. The Briefing expects a shakeout, and it is right. Too many vendors are selling the same AI to the same buyer. The model owners and the deep workflow owners survive. The middle is squeezed.

Source: The Information, The Briefing and AI Agenda, Sep 23, 2026

The middle hollows out, in people too

Nikunj Kothari describes a founder who hires only two kinds of people: AI-native, high-agency people with no fixed habits, and deep experts who design the system they run on. He avoids the AI-curious middle that falls back on old patterns.

His argument: AI alone does not amplify you, and agency alone no longer does either. Combine them and you are hard to stop. For investors, the implication is smaller teams, lower check sizes for the same revenue, and a screen for rate of learning over resume. It applies to investors as well.

Source: Nikunj Kothari, The Amplification Gap, Sep 23, 2026

Briefly

Kalshi's clearinghouse asked the CFTC to allow margin on some event contracts, with full collateral when binary risk peaks. Retail pays in full. Hedgers want capital efficiency. Margin is how a betting app becomes a derivatives venue, and the CFTC is now the gatekeeper for new tradeable risk, as with GPU futures.

Mirendil, from former Anthropic researchers, is in talks to raise up to $1B at $5B. Snorkel AI raised $350M at $3.5B. Heidi, the Australian medical scribe, raised $340M to expand in Asia and the Middle East. CoreWeave closed a $4.2B convertible. Anthropic is in early talks to lease up to 1GW from Apollo-owned Stream. Australia's prime minister said an OpenAI agent accessed nonpublic files on a government site, a liability nobody has priced.
