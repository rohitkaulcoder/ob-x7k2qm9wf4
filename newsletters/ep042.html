The best model is not the best business

Anthropic told investors that third-quarter revenue rose 46% to $16.8 billion from $11.5 billion in the June quarter, per the FT as relayed by The Information. The Information's Martin Peers says the numbers imply slower growth than the second quarter, which is likely to cap its IPO valuation. Bankers floated $2 trillion. Investors now talk of $1.4 to $1.5 trillion. One said the IPO "will need to price to sell."

Illustrative arithmetic: $16.8 billion times four is $67.2 billion. At $1.5 trillion that is about 22 times annualised revenue, and at $2 trillion nearly 30. The argument is really about whether coming quarters look like 46% growth or less.

This is not really about Anthropic. It is about moats in a market where good enough is cheap. Peers cites TrustedRouter listing 665 models, mostly open source, Chinese firms building their own, reports that Microsoft cut spending on Anthropic models, and over $500 billion of compute commitments over a decade. Fixed costs that size make slower growth costly.

The counter-bet is Apple. Per Koyfin data cited by The Information, Apple is up 26% this year against 10 to 12% for Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft, and is the only one of them whose valuation multiple has risen. It closed above $5 trillion on Wednesday. Bloomberg adds that a September call by AI chiefs for a slowdown has not slowed anyone: new models from Anthropic, Meta and Google arrived within weeks. Nobody can afford to be the one who slows.

The IGBT: a switch built from two flawed switches

Asianometry traces the insulated-gate bipolar transistor. A power semiconductor is a switch for large currents and voltages, with no moving parts. A good one conducts like a metal when on, blocks like an insulator when off, and flips fast.

The two parents each fail on one count. A power BJT floods its middle with electrons and holes, which lowers resistance (conductivity modulation), but it needs steady drive current and is slow to turn off. A power MOSFET is voltage-controlled, fast and rugged, but handles high voltage poorly. B. Jayant Baliga at General Electric proposed merging them: a MOSFET gate controlling a BJT-like body. Illustrative arithmetic: at 100 amps, a 0.2 ohm resistance loses 2,000 watts as heat (current squared times resistance). A device behaving like a 2 volt drop loses 200.

The merged device nearly defeated itself. Its layered structure includes a parasitic transistor that can create latch-up: a self-sustaining loop where the gate no longer controls the device. Baliga used a deep P+ region and irradiation to cut latch-up and speed switching. Toshiba's Akio Nakagawa then lowered the saturation current below the latch-up current, so the device tops out before it can latch. Illustratively, latch-up at 300 amps and a ceiling at 200 means the danger zone is never reached.

The invention credit is disputed between Baliga, RCA's COMFET work and Nakagawa's saturation fix, and the newsletter lays out the competing claims. The lesson is a business one: the winner made a flaw harmless rather than removing it. IGBTs sit in air conditioners, wind turbines and EV motor drives. The newsletter covers the device and history, not current market share.

Agents need a payment policy, not a credential

A card number does two jobs: it identifies the paying account and it grants authority to start a payment. Tokenisation separates them, which is why a subscription survives a card replacement. a16z's Wallet Wars piece argues agents separate them further. Four contests follow: connect, reach, routing and liability. Stripe's Link, Shopify's Shop Pay and PayPal already hold credentials at scale, and Link can issue a single-use virtual card for merchants that have not integrated.

The sharpest point is the difference between payment interoperability and relationship interoperability. With a virtual card, two transactions occur: you pay the wallet, and the wallet pays the merchant. The money arrives. The merchant relationship and card rewards may not.

Liability is unresolved. If you authorise $500 for a hotel and the agent books wrong dates within budget, the hotel delivered what was ordered. The piece says card networks are developing scoped credentials that preserve chargeback rights. The India implication, our inference and not the piece's: UPI already provides credentials and rails at scale, so the open layer may be policy and disputes.

ASML may be building a hybrid bonder

SemiAnalysis infers that ASML is building a die-to-wafer hybrid bonding tool, from bonding patent families filed from December 2022 to November 2024, a report and ASML's roadmap. ASML has announced nothing. Hybrid bonding joins silicon directly, copper pad to copper pad, with no solder bumps, allowing tighter stacking. Die-to-wafer is the hard version, and Besi is the incumbent with volume tools.

SemiAnalysis expects ASML's edge to be alignment and particle control, the skills of lithography, and expects advanced logic to adopt first. It calls this bad news for Besi. It is an analyst's inference, read only in part. The shift it points to is real either way: gains come less from shrinking transistors than from joining silicon.

SemiAnalysis, Oct 15 (free portion read; later sections may require a subscription).

India: fewer seats, higher fares, and a political stress test

Domestic air passenger traffic fell 6.3% in August from a year earlier, per DGCA data compiled by ICRA and reported by The Core. Cleartrip's Gaurav Patwari says it is mainly a supply problem: fewer seats and higher fares. The Sensex closed Wednesday at 72,781.89, down 0.34%, and the Nifty 50 at 22,622.70, down 0.44%.

The Economist's Essential India reports that Delhi saw its most sweeping crackdown on peaceful protest in years, with protesters demanding action over a voter-roll verification drive in which, it says, about 130 million names were deleted. We report the newsletter's account with attribution. The point for investors is narrow: institutional and political risk is an input.
