The Fed is about to hike into an oil shock

Consumer prices rose 3.4% in August, and rate futures moved from 70% to 85% odds of a quarter-point Fed hike next week - the first increase in three years, and the market is pricing more than one. This is Kevin Warsh's Fed, and it is staring at an oil shock rather than a demand problem.

The supply side got worse the same night: Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline (the 7-million-barrel-a-day bypass around the Strait of Hormuz) after it came under attack, while Houthi militants seized critical territory along the Bab al-Mandeb strait. Both chokepoints compromised at once. Brent traded above $104, with analysts now saying $120 out loud.

Every story this week - Oracle's prepayments, the $7.5T buildout, the AI IPO queue - was priced on cheap-ish capital and expanding multiples. A hike into 3.4% inflation with $120 crude flips both: discount rates rise, and the AI trade is the longest-duration trade on the board.

  • For India: ~90% imported oil means $120 crude is a direct consumer tax, rupee pressure, and an RBI dilemma it didn't want.
  • Bloomberg's consumer sentiment read - the first interesting one in ages - shows even Republicans souring; broad pain is usually when the Fed blinks, or breaks something.
  • Watch next week's FOMC like a hawk.

Nvidia's $530B backstop universe

SemiAnalysis mapped Nvidia's latest 10-Q and found $530B of gross off-balance-sheet guarantees, up from $184B a single quarter earlier. The breakdown: supply and capacity commitments jumped $119B to $279B (mostly memory, 96% due by fiscal 2029); guarantees exploded $3.5B to $108.5B covering SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio - 4.25 GW leased to OpenAI for twenty years, with Nvidia guaranteeing the land, power, and shell; and two line items appeared for the first time - $36B of take-or-pay floors under neocloud capacity and $20B of data center leases Nvidia signed as tenant.

Against that: $91B on-balance-sheet liabilities, $22B cash, and an investment book that grew from $45B to $128B, throwing off $23.7B of first-half gains. Heads I win, tails... who loses?

Stitched with the week's other stories - Levine's AAA AI debt, Oracle's customer pre-funding - Nvidia has become the central bank of the AI buildout, backstopping every layer while its equity book gains depend on the same demand those guarantees underwrite.

  • The $279B memory commitment is its own signal: the real 2027 constraint is HBM, not logic.
  • For Indian founders: none of this risk sits on Indian balance sheets - just don't sign take-or-pay floors at the top of the cycle.
Source: SemiAnalysis, Sep 11, 2026 · read the original

Recursive self-improvement vs the sanity caucus

Dwarkesh convened John Schulman (co-inventor of RLHF), Beren Millidge, and Charlie O'Neill to debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement - AI that improves the AI. Exactly what Jacob Coxon warned about when he walked out of Anthropic, and after OpenAI's agents solved a decades-old math problem this week, the question has stopped being philosophy.

The counterweight arrived the same night: Alex Kantrowitz at Big Technology argues the risk conversation has gone off the rails. His first point: the risk of AI killing humanity is completely unquantifiable - a researcher's "10%" is a costume on a vibe, not math. His second: many in Silicon Valley earnestly believe the risk is real, and dismissing them as marketing is lazy. Both things can be true.

Meanwhile Newcomer finds that startups actually building in AI price cybersecurity far above existential risk - the boring, patchable, 50-day-late sandbox vulnerabilities. The market for actual problems is functioning.

  • Read both sides back to back - the timeline debate and the sanity check are the two poles of the only conversation that matters this fall.
Source: Dwarkesh Podcast; Big Technology; Newcomer, Sep 11-12, 2026 · read the original

Instinct's compute crunch makes the news

The Information's exclusive: Instinct - the personal AI assistant this very show is built on - is facing a compute crunch that could lead to new funding. The follow-up adds the number: founder Noah Shinn has told prospective investors he wants to raise as much as $1B for compute.

The economics question underneath is the sharp one: consumer AI has hit the wall where growth itself requires more compute, and unlike a social app, every new user costs real money to serve from day one. A $20-30 subscription cannot fund frontier-scale compute for power users - so prices rise, usage gets rationed, or the company raises a billion and figures it out later. It's the OpenAI shape at consumer scale, in the same week OpenAI suspended its own $200 tier on capacity.

Our read: capacity-constrained consumer AI with real retention is the best funding problem to have, and a billion-dollar raise would price the category properly for the first time.

Salesforce vs Figma: is the UI dead?

The full Information piece behind yesterday's headline: Salesforce is betting agents eat the interface - point your agent, skip the app. Figma is betting the opposite: design and craft matter more when anyone can generate software. Two smart companies, opposite bets on the same question.

Our take sits with Figma, reluctantly: taste is the moat when generation is free.
