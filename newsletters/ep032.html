Ep 32: Who Pays for the Meter

A subscription is a marketing expense with a meter on it

SemiAnalysis estimates that for Anthropic, subscriptions are about 10% of revenue but can take over 40% of inference compute, lowering blended revenue per megawatt by roughly $36M. The plans are subsidized on purpose. They buy customers the way a gym membership does, by betting most users under-use them.

The interesting part is how anyone knows. Subscription plans show a 0-100% usage meter over 5-hour and 7-day windows, not a price per token. SemiAnalysis runs repeated calls that isolate one token type at a time (fresh input, cache writes, cache reads, output), records the running token total each time the meter ticks, drops the first and last partial steps, and divides meter movement by tokens used. That gives an API-equivalent value per plan, model and workload.

Our toy arithmetic, not theirs: if 40 of 100 compute units earn 10% of revenue, the other 60 earn 90%, so a unit sold per token to a business earns about 3.5 times a unit sold flat to a person. For founders building on these models, the risk is that limits can change by editing a credit table. OpenAI cut usage on its $200 plan in half and dropped relative-usage language.

Source: SemiAnalysis, "Anthropic Subscriptions Offer 5x+ More Value Than OpenAI," Oct 5, 2026 (email excerpt only). Paywalled for readers.

Claude's share is real. So is the plateau

Big Technology, using Similarweb and Apptopia data, reports Claude's share of daily active users across AI apps rose from 1.4% in January to 14.9% in September, while ChatGPT's fell from 41.8% to 30.7%. Claude's site drew 950 million visits in August, up about 540% year on year. Claude led on minutes per daily user in September at 27, against Grok at 23.4 and ChatGPT at 23.1. Enterprise SSO traffic is up roughly 4x since March.

The caveats matter. US downloads peaked at 7.42 million in March and fell to 3.26 million in September. These are third-party panel estimates. And over 80% of users actively use only one chatbot, so the market is a contest of defaults. Gaining share means taking someone's default, and the people left are the ones who like theirs.

Source: Big Technology (Marty Swant and Alex Kantrowitz), Oct 5, 2026.

The reverse acqui-hire is a merger with the paperwork rearranged

Matt Levine lays out the structure. The acquirer hires the startup's key people, gets a board waiver, takes a non-exclusive license to the technology, and pays the startup a large sum for both. The startup becomes a shell holding cash, which it pays out. Because nothing is bought, there is no Hart-Scott-Rodino filing (the threshold is $133.9 million) and probably no shareholder vote.

This is not really about Nvidia. It is about whether asking permission or restructuring is cheaper. The FTC chair has said the agency is starting to examine these deals, and the New York Times reported the Justice Department is investigating whether Nvidia skirted review in its Groq deal, where Groq's CEO and COO joined Nvidia under a non-exclusive license. For early-stage investors, the exit becomes a payout from a shell, so the distribution terms matter, and a regulatory crackdown could slow strategic exits.

Source: Matt Levine, Money Stuff, Oct 5, 2026 (Bloomberg Opinion), citing Bloomberg News and the New York Times.

AI's liability is the number no model holds

Martin Peers lists OpenAI's pile-on: a New York City Council hearing on AI risk, a Senate investigation led by Josh Hawley, an FTC inquiry, inquiries in Australia and Europe, a Florida court action, and a nonprofit's lawsuit over the Hugging Face hack, which he thinks has a thin standing argument. His point is that the futures of OpenAI and Anthropic depend on liability as well as costs and revenue. If a rogue agent does serious damage, the exposure could be enormous.

Markets are calm. Nvidia closed at a record $238.90 on Monday, up 28% this year, with a $5.76 trillion market cap, about $900 billion above Apple. Also noted: Reflection AI's first open-weight model, and OpenAI's test of a visual ad format with US advertisers later this month.

Source: The Information, The Briefing (Martin Peers), Oct 5, 2026. Paywalled for readers.

India: foreign money reads strength as a reason to sell

Foreign portfolio investors sold $2.1 billion of Indian equities in September, ending two months of net buying, and $1.1 billion of index-eligible sovereign bonds left, the worst since March. Crude is around $102 a barrel. Indian companies raised a record Rs 2.43 trillion (about $25.3 billion) in the first half of fiscal 2027, up 75%, with average listing gains rising from 7% to 19%.

Citing Bernstein, Govindraj Ethiraj says foreign flows tracked GDP growth until 2007 and the correlation is now negative. The supply of new equity is part of the story. For a venture fund, a hot IPO window is the exit. For foreign money, it is more paper to absorb.

Source: The Core, Oct 5, 2026, citing Bloomberg, PRIME Database and Bernstein.
