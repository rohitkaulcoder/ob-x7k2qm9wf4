Ep 17: The Memory That Arrived Too Early

Overnight Brief, written edition · Mon, Sep 21, 2026 · audio: 18:00
🎧 Listen to the audio episode

A working technology can still lack a market

Optane promised a layer between fast, volatile DRAM and cheap, persistent NAND. The memory cells worked. The system did not converge around them.

The less software changed, the less value Optane delivered. The more value customers wanted, the more processors, motherboards and applications they had to change. Intel cited 582 proofs of concept in 2020, but only 40% used the more valuable App Direct mode.

This was not really a memory problem. It was a coordination problem wearing a DIMM.

The category was squeezed from both sides

Micron expected Optane-like memory to cost half as much per gigabyte as DRAM and four to five times as much as NAND. It needed workloads that were too expensive in DRAM and too slow on flash.

Then 3D NAND followed one of the steepest cost curves in semiconductor history. DRAM improved. The neighbours moved inward.

Intel and Micron invested roughly $6B in their shared fab. Micron said under-utilisation cost $400M a year. Volume needed adoption; adoption needed low cost and supply confidence; both required volume. Micron exited in 2021. Intel wound down Optane in 2022.

Standards can matter more than cell chemistry

Optane asked buyers to accept a proprietary medium, one CPU family, specialised hardware and application changes. Single sourcing made infrastructure buyers nervous.

CXL attacks a related memory problem through a standard interface for pooling capacity across processors and devices. Many suppliers can compete behind it. The ecosystem does not have to bet on one factory.

Deep technology is a coordinated market: materials, yields, controllers, protocols, operating systems, tools and anchor workloads. A working die is the entry ticket.

Nvidia becomes a power scheduler

Nvidia tracks available power and data-centre shells worldwide, invests in energy-linked companies and has signed $20B of leases intended for customers. A sold GPU earns nothing while it waits for a substation.

Its MaxLPS system spreads workloads across time to avoid coincident power spikes. Nvidia says this can fit 40% more GPUs under the same electrical allocation. The grid connection becomes a scheduling problem.

Compute will develop service classes. Batch training takes interruptible power. Real-time inference pays for firm capacity. The data centre starts behaving like an energy trader with a compiler attached.

The weakest signature prices the campus

Zenith Arc sold $2.25B of bonds to build a 149MW Oklahoma data centre for Jane Street. The bonds began with an 8.875% coupon. Their market yield has risen to roughly 11.3%.

Jane Street guarantees the lease but carries a BB rating. The project debt is capped by the tenant whose rent services it. A comparable project with a Google guarantee priced at a much tighter spread.

The servers may be novel. The lender asks an old question: who pays if the plan changes? The marginal cost of AI is set partly by the weakest credible name on the lease.

The IPO celebration hides the invoice

Long-run IPO data cited by Mostly Metrics shows $250B left on the table, about 21% of all capital raised. A recent tech sample left roughly 25 cents for every dollar of proceeds.

A modest discount pays investors for uncertainty. Day-one scarcity can also exaggerate demand. But incentives matter. Bankers want happy clients. Executives see their remaining stake rise. The diffuse loser is the balance sheet that sold shares too cheaply.

“The stock went up” is good news for holders. It is not evidence the issuance was priced well.

Agents need state, not better prose

Go-to-market software is moving from copilots that draft and score to agents that research accounts, act on signals and update the revenue system.

The hard part is not an email. It is durable state. Did this prospect decline? Is contact allowed? Which product fits? Did the meeting alter the forecast? Identity, permission and audit trails matter more than eloquence.

Point tools get squeezed when one system owns the workflow. Systems of record remain because the agent still needs canonical truth. The valuable layer acts and leaves evidence.
