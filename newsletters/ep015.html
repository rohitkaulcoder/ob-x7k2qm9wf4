The IPO is a controlled spillway

Anthropic is considering letting existing shareholders sell in its IPO while locking up some remaining shares for longer than the usual 180 days. The company was valued below $20B two years ago and at $965B in May. Bankers have discussed $1.5T.

At that scale, insider liquidity becomes market structure. One percent of a $1.5T company is $15B. Selling some shares inside the offering lets Anthropic place them with chosen institutions instead of waiting for a flood when the lockup expires.

The choreography is delicate. Public investors must believe Anthropic needs new capital for extraordinary demand and that old holders taking liquidity are not calling the top.

Fifteen San Franciscos of compute

Anthropic has agreed to access at least 14.8GW of compute since October. Announced commitments could cost up to $517B, mostly over the next decade. Google and Amazon account for 11GW and more than $300B; Microsoft gets at least $30B; SpaceX could receive $1.25B a month through May 2029.

Run continuously, 14.8GW consumes about 130 terawatt-hours a year before cooling overhead. The Information compares one gigawatt to San Francisco. Anthropic's pipeline is roughly fifteen San Franciscos.

Reported annualised revenue is above $65B. The commitments span years and some can be cancelled, so this is not an accounting ratio. It is the bet public investors must underwrite: tomorrow's fixed capacity against today's explosive revenue.

Can the labs police themselves?

Anthropic, OpenAI and Google have reportedly discussed an industry standards body since July. Dario Amodei wants outside evaluators to get employee-like model access; Sam Altman supports testing and auditing but thinks the labs may have to organise it without Washington.

The principal-agent problem is immediate. The supervised firms choose the supervisor, control its access and lose billions if an adverse finding delays a release. Independence means the ability to publish an unwelcome result.

There is a competition problem too. A costly testing regime may reduce risk and become a moat. A $100M compliance bill is trivial beside Anthropic's compute budget and fatal to a startup. The right questions are who writes the rules, who audits the writers, and whether entrants can comply without incumbent permission.

189GiB in the slower lane

DeepSeek's Engram adds learned lookups for recurring multi-token patterns. The table is about 189GiB, but each token position requests only 12.4KiB across the model. The addresses depend on token IDs known early, so a server can prefetch rows from host DRAM while earlier layers compute.

SemiAnalysis found that returning the table to HBM did not materially improve B300 results. DRAM offload could reduce tensor parallelism from four GPUs to two and improve the performance frontier by up to 1.6 times. The sparse lookup got slower; the whole system got better because HBM was freed for KV cache and fewer GPUs served each replica.

This separates capacity from bandwidth. If a small read can be predicted early enough to hide its trip, every byte need not live in the fastest tier. HBM demand does not vanish, but system software and ordinary memory capture more value.

Why cheaper SSD lost

Near 125 tokens a second per user, SemiAnalysis measured 121M total tokens per dollar with DRAM and 52M with SSD. A warm filesystem cache still paid for copying row IDs to the CPU, deduplicating, gathering rows, pinned buffers, GPU transfer and dequantisation.

Cheaper storage creates value only if it removes or better uses an expensive component. DRAM offload freed HBM and sometimes GPUs. SSD left the same four expensive GPUs in place and added coordination overhead.

Engram is also not a detachable dictionary. Removing it changes downstream expert routing. Memory and reasoning are entangled, so serving optimisations must match the trained architecture.

Salesforce accepts someone else's front door

Salesforce is leaning into Anthropic and OpenAI chatbots as the interface to customer workflows, according to Stratechery. The assistant may replace the dashboards and menus where enterprise software controlled the relationship.

The analogy is online travel. Aircraft and reservation systems remained essential, but the comparison box directed demand. An agent asked to update a forecast and draft outreach can route across CRM, email, billing and a warehouse without the employee consciously choosing any one application.

Salesforce can charge for trusted data, permissions, audit trails and reliable actions underneath. The risk is becoming interchangeable plumbing behind another company's brand. Integration is probably wiser than denial. It is still a concession about where pricing power moves.

AI's capital stack shares one fault line

Middle Eastern sovereign money may represent as much as a quarter of global AI investment by one estimate cited by Newcomer. Oil exports are reportedly down by half or more amid conflict, though higher prices offset some volume. Saudi Arabia still pledged $15B for domestic AI; Qatar has pulled back.

Higher rates raise data-centre debt costs. Conflict raises physical and insurance costs. AWS acknowledged permanent customer-data loss after drone attacks on facilities in Bahrain and the UAE. These pressures are correlated: oil funds sovereign investors, sovereign investors fund labs, and lenders finance infrastructure.

A $517B announcement is not cash in a bank. It is contracts, cancellation clauses, future revenue and lender confidence. AI usage can transform the economy while individual financing structures still break. The hard question is who owns the capacity when prices fall and capital gets selective.
