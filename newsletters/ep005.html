OpenAI starts a price war

At Goldman's Communacopia conference in San Francisco, OpenAI finance chief Sarah Friar told a standing-room-only ballroom that the price cut on the GPT 5.6 Luna model drove a tenfold increase in usage - giving OpenAI the highest market share on OpenRouter, the routing service developers use to hop between models. The Information had already reported Luna doing more than twice the token volume of Anthropic's Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 combined on that platform; the usage surge more than paid for the cut.

The timing is the story: Anthropic's IPO paperwork is expected any week, and its claim to fame is a reported $65B annualized revenue rate as the premium, high-end provider - and premiums are exactly what a price war eats. Anthropic has already blinked once: Fable 5.1 costs 25% less than Fable 5 for typical workloads.

Layer in the Ironwood benchmark from the day before - TPU serving at $0.18 per million tokens against Nvidia's $0.22-0.28 - and the cost of intelligence is falling on both axes at once: cheaper silicon per token, and labs cutting token prices to buy share. Great for application margins, brutal for anyone whose moat was "we have the best model."

  • Going public as the expensive option while your rival demonstrates 10x volume elasticity is a tough S-1 narrative. Anthropic's answer has to be that enterprises pay for trust, not tokens - the next month of OpenRouter share data will say whether that's true.

Data beat the architects, three to one

Dwarkesh Patel's team asked a deceptively simple question: since 2019, how much of AI progress came from better model architectures versus better training data? They trained the full grid - 2019 recipes on 2025 data, 2025 recipes on 2019 data - up to 10^19 FLOPs.

The result: data improvements delivered 12x in compute-efficiency gains; model improvements, 3.7x. And the two are mostly independent - 88% of the variance is just the two effects added together.

The caveat everyone will skip: the model innovations of the last six years weren't mainly about efficiency, they made scale possible at all - mixtures of experts, FlashAttention, stability tricks. Their analogy: 2019 models were sailboats, today's are container ships. Once you have a container ship you stop curating cargo obsessively, because aggressive filtering means training on the same data dozens of times, which hurts. Data curation matters most exactly when your model is small.

  • Investor read: quietly bullish for labs with the best proprietary data flywheels, a reality check on "our novel architecture" pitch decks, and - Jevons again - evidence that progress came from the cheap stuff even as the buildout bets on compute scarcity.

Levine on Robinhood's tokenized AMC

Matt Levine took on Robinhood offering tokenized AMC - yes, the meme stock - to European customers. The surface story is silly; the deep story is two theories of what a share even is. Theory one: a share is an entry on the company's own ledger, and nobody should manufacture one except the company. Theory two, Levine's own loosey-goosey view: a share is a bundle of economic exposures, and finance has always let intermediaries reslice that bundle - depositary receipts, swaps, CFDs. A token is the newest wrapper on the oldest trick.

The regulatory question - what does the token holder actually own, economically and legally - is the same question as the Tether freeze story: what process is owed to whom when the wrapper meets the state. And when the wrapper lands in India, where the securities-law answer to "who may issue a claim on a share" is emphatically theory one, the SEBI conversation will be fascinating.

Chime buys its bank

Chime is buying its longtime banking partner, Stride Bank, for $590M in cash - seven years of partnership turned into vertical integration, and the stock jumped 10% after hours. Fintech's middlemen keep getting bought by their biggest customers.

If your neobank depends on a partner bank, that partner is either an acquisition target or a risk. There is no third state.

iPhone Fold day

Tonight was the Apple event: John Ternus's first keynote as CEO, and by every leak tracker the most radically different iPhone lineup in the company's history. The foldable was real, name still a coin flip - iPhone Fold, Ultra, or Duo - with Apple entering foldables nearly a decade after Samsung's first Galaxy Fold: not first, just inevitable.

The context: record financials, a full slate, Cook watching from the executive chairman's office. The product was baked under Cook; what was being graded was Ternus's pace and taste. And looming over it all: Huawei's $3,000 foldable already exists. Our framing test: if Apple pitched the foldable as an AI device - a bigger canvas for agentic workflows - the Cook-to-Ternus pivot is real; if it was just a nicer screen, the pivot is cosmetic. Builders' corner: how Grok Bot got built

Lenny has the other side of yesterday's story (Claire firing her whole OpenClaw stack for Grok Bot): Roman Ugarte - who led growth at Cursor from 15 people to over a thousand, through its acquisition by SpaceX - helped incubate Grok Bot inside SpaceX AI. First line of code to working internal product in four weeks, public launch three weeks later.

Two details worth stealing: they built it from scratch rather than bolting it onto Cursor, and the team personally onboarded nearly 300 of its first users - the unscalable thing, done deliberately, at the company that builds rockets. Their product philosophy has a name: colleague-pilled. The agent should feel like a colleague, not a tool - exactly the "every bot is a new hire" framing from the user side. When builder and power user independently converge on the same mental model, that's a product truth.

What happens to code review when agents write the code

The Pragmatic Engineer on the question haunting every CTO: pull requests are bigger, faster, and endless, and the industry's answers are sorting into camps. Most popular: AI reviews the code, humans review the review. Then triage by blast radius - low-risk changes skip human review, high-risk don't (OpenAI and Anthropic both run this internally). Some teams review the plan and tests but not the implementation. A brave few are talking about dropping human review entirely. The harness is the product everywhere now, even in your pull queue.

  • Also in builders' news: a transcription model war - Google's Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, Wispr's Canto, and Meta's Muse Voice Transcribe (its first real-time model, 25 languages, up to 20 speakers, adaptive compute on hard words). Speech is becoming the cheapest, most contested layer of the stack.
Source: The Pragmatic Engineer; Week in Voice AI, Sep 8, 2026 · read the original
